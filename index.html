AT&T

Technical Product Manager, Salesforce Service Platform, 2025–present, Dallas TX

I own backend service capabilities (fraud, authentication, billing and search) on Salesforce Service Cloud, and I'm moving call-centre and retail teams off Opus, the legacy platform, onto modern, AI-ready tools.

One store app before the holiday peak

Store reps juggled Salesforce and Opus on 8–10 second pages. I led the move of 31 features into one React Native app with an agentic experience, across 5 teams in two months.

~70% faster8–10s → 2–3s page load
31 featuresin 2 months, 5 teams (~50 people)
~5,000 storesbuilt for national rollout
⚡ Feel the difference

Typical page load: 8–10s before, 2–3s after.

Read the full story

The problem

  • Reps had no single place to finish a customer interaction.
  • Slow pages, repeated across thousands of store visits a day.
  • Agentic features need a fast, modern front end, and the old stack couldn't provide one.

Key decisions

  • Two migration paths, chosen per feature. 16 back-office flows, such as lead management, moved as they were and are linked from the new experience. 15 customer-facing flows, such as customer check-in, were redesigned around the agent.
💡 The rule: redesign the moments that shape the customer's visit, and move back-office flows as they are.
  • New front end, same proven data layer. The app uses existing Salesforce objects, which connect through MuleSoft to backend APIs. Keeping the backend made two months realistic; the speed came from replacing heavy pages with a lightweight app.
  • Agents only where reasoning adds value: cross-sell suggestions, customer summaries and bill explanations, with sources shown.
  • Pilot before scale: launched in pilot stores first to catch issues before national rollout.

What I'd do next

  • Redesign the 16 lift-and-shift features into the new workflow, and retire the second experience.
  • Measure containment, cross-sell acceptance and time per visit as the rollout scales.

Neuf, the AI startup I co-founded

Co-Founder & Product Leader, Engage AI, 2023–present (advisory since 2025), remote

From resume scorer to AI-powered applicant tracking system

Small recruiting agencies can't grow without hiring more recruiters. Neuf started as a resume scorer; customers kept asking for the whole hiring process in one place, so we rebuilt it as a full platform. I owned product and technology end to end and worked on sales with my co-founder.

5 payingcustomers + 10 on free licenses
0 lostin the pivot to a full platform
3 devshired, independent in 2 months
Read the full story

Key decisions

  • Explainable scoring: 11 dimensions with reasons for each, so recruiters can challenge the result.
  • Privacy built in: contact details and profile links are removed before data reaches the model, and names are never sent.
  • Manipulation protection: detects hidden text written to game AI scoring.
  • An assistant with limits: searches all applicants with tools, reasons over the top results, refuses protected-characteristic questions and is read-only.
  • Cost-aware AI: a stronger model for deep scoring, a cheaper one for bulk tasks, and usage capped per plan.
  • Self-serve billing with Stripe subscriptions.

What I'd do next

  • Launch AI video-interview evaluation, already in the pipeline.
  • Measure how often recruiters agree with the AI's top picks.
  • Let the assistant draft outreach for recruiter approval.

Canadian Tire

Technical Product Manager, One Digital Platform, 2022–2023, Canada / US

One platform for five retail brands

Sport Chek, Mark's, Party City and two more brands each ran on a monolith with point-to-point integrations. The app was slow, inventory was often wrong and pricing relied on manual work. I led the move to one shared digital platform.

5 brandson one shared platform
Fewer errorsin quotes after the move to Salesforce CPQ
2 monthsto launch a new home-installation partner
Read the full story

The problem

  • Each brand's stack was separate, so every fix and feature had to be built more than once.
  • A monolith with ESB integrations made the app slow and inventory unreliable.
  • Pricing and quoting relied on manual work, which caused errors and rework.

Key decisions

  • Cloud microservices with caching, so the app loaded faster and showed accurate inventory across all five brands.
  • A rules engine for inventory-based pricing, and the pricing engine migrated to Salesforce CPQ to cut quote errors and rework.
  • Partner switch under a deadline. I delivered a quote-to-order-to-payment flow for home-installation services with a new third-party partner in two months.
💡 The rule: launch like-for-like scope first to hit the date, then phase in improvements such as reporting.

What I'd do next

  • Track quote accuracy and time-to-quote per brand to show where the shared platform pays off most.

Loblaw Digital

Technical Product Manager, PC Express, 2021–2022, Canada

Grocery fulfilment at national scale

PC Express's legacy fulfilment platform was slow, prone to downtime and couldn't keep up with online grocery demand. I built the order orchestration layer and rolled the new platform out across the store network.

~35% fasterorder-to-delivery than legacy
~1,200 storesrolled out in phases
SQL-ledpick-time analysis for operations
Read the full story

The problem

  • A monolith caused downtime at the moments demand peaked.
  • Orders weren't broken down efficiently for the people picking them in store.

Key decisions

  • An API orchestration layer for order management that splits each order from SAP Commerce and routes it to the right store, aisle and individual picker.
  • Monolith to microservices, to reduce downtime and let parts of the system scale independently.
  • Phased rollout across around 1,200 stores, so issues surfaced early in a small group.
  • Data-driven operations: SQL analysis of pick times guided staffing and process changes.

What I'd do next

  • Feed pick-time data back into routing, so orders go to the fastest available picker.
